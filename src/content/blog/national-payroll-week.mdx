National Payroll Week runs September 7–11 this year, which most people will spend not thinking about payroll at all. That's the point. That's the whole job.

Payroll is one of the few functions where doing everything right produces no signal. The money lands. The withholding is correct. The new hire's first check isn't short. The person who moved states in June has the right tax setup. Nobody sends an email about any of that, because there's nothing to send an email about.

The only time payroll becomes visible is when something breaks — and then it's visible to everyone, immediately, personally. It's a job where the feedback loop only fires in one direction.

### Recognition has to be deliberate, because the work isn't visible

Most teams get thanked in the natural flow of things. Someone ships a feature and there's a demo. Someone closes a deal and there's a Slack channel with a party emoji. The work announces itself.

Payroll doesn't announce itself, so appreciation for it has to be scheduled the way the pay runs are. If you wait for a natural moment, you'll wait forever — the natural moment is a mistake, and by then thanks isn't what's needed.

### Name the thing that didn't go wrong

Generic thanks lands especially flat here, because "thanks for all you do" is exactly the sentiment that has always failed to arrive. Try naming something real:

The quarter you moved to a new provider and nobody's check was late. The open enrollment questions you answered one at a time instead of pointing at the PDF. The person you caught being taxed in the wrong state before they found out at filing time.

That's the kind of thing a payroll team remembers, because it's proof someone was actually paying attention to work designed to be invisible.
