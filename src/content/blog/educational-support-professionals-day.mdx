Educational Support Professionals Day is Wednesday, November 18 — the third Wednesday of November, and probably the least-known date on the school appreciation calendar.

Teachers get a full week in May, and deservedly. Educational support professionals get this one Wednesday, and most schools don't mark it, and most parents have never heard of it.

Which is odd, given who's on the list. Bus drivers. Paraprofessionals and classroom aides. Cafeteria staff. Front office secretaries. Custodians. Library and media assistants. Crossing guards.

### They bracket the day

Think about a kid's actual school day from end to end. The first adult they encounter is usually the bus driver. The last one is usually the bus driver. In between, the person who knows their name at the front desk, the person who notices they're not eating lunch, the aide who sits with them through the part of the day they can't do alone.

That's an enormous amount of contact with children, held by people who are mostly outside the recognition structures built for teachers.

### Aides in particular

Paraprofessionals often work one-to-one with the students who need the most, which means their work is simultaneously the most intensive in the building and the least visible outside one classroom. A parent whose kid had a good year because of a specific aide frequently doesn't know the aide's role, their hours, or that November 18 is a thing.

They usually do know exactly what changed, though. That's the message worth sending.

### The thing that gets remembered

Same as everywhere else on this calendar: specificity. "Thank you for all you do" is a form letter. "You're the reason he stopped dreading Tuesdays" is not.

Front office staff who fielded twelve panicked phone calls. The driver who noticed a kid was upset and mentioned it to someone. The cafeteria manager who quietly made sure a family's situation never became a conversation their child had to have.
