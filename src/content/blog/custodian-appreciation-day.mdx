Custodian Appreciation Day is Friday, October 2. The reason it's easy to forget is also the reason it matters: most people never share a room with the person the day is about.

Custodial work happens in the gap. The building empties at six, and the work starts. Or it starts at five in the morning and is finished before the first person arrives. The result is visible every single day — a clean floor, a stocked bathroom, a classroom reset — and the person responsible is, by design, not there when you notice it.

### Gratitude that depends on running into someone doesn't happen

Most workplace appreciation is opportunistic. You pass someone in the hall and say thanks. You're in the same meeting and mention the thing they did. It costs nothing because the moment presents itself.

Take away the shared hours and that entire mechanism disappears. It's not that people feel less grateful toward the custodian who's kept their building running for nine years — it's that there is no hallway where that sentence could get said.

Which means the appreciation has to be asynchronous or it doesn't exist at all.

### Specific beats grand

A plaque is a fine gesture and a forgettable one. What people in this kind of work tend to remember is evidence that someone noticed the particular thing:

The winter the heat failed and you were in at four in the morning salting the walk. The way you always leave the third-floor break room set up for the early crew. The fact that you learned every kid's name in a building where most adults don't.

That's not flattery, it's observation — and observation is the thing that's actually scarce here.
