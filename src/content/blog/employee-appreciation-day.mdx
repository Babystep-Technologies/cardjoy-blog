title: 'One Day a Year Is the Wrong Unit'
description: 'Employee Appreciation Day is March 5. It works fine as a starting line and badly as a substitute for the other 364.'

---

Employee Appreciation Day is Friday, March 5 — the first Friday in March, as always.

We've written before about why [recognition doesn't need a budget line](/blog/recognition-doesnt-need-a-budget-line/): the advice that keeps surfacing from people who actually run these programs is that frequency beats size, and specificity beats both. Employee Appreciation Day is a direct test of that idea, because it's the single most concentrated recognition event on the corporate calendar and it's built on the opposite premise.

### The competition problem

A once-a-year event has to compete with eleven months of silence, and it loses. If the only structured appreciation your company produces arrives on the first Friday in March, the message people take from it isn't "we're valued." It's "it's March."

That's not an argument against the day. It's an argument against the day being the whole program.

### What the day is actually good for

Treat it as a forcing function rather than a celebration. It's a scheduled moment when managers who intended to say something and didn't have an easy excuse to finally do it — and one honest, specific note delivered on March 5 is worth more than any amount of catering.

The useful version of the question isn't "what should we do for Employee Appreciation Day." It's "who on this team hasn't heard anything specific from me since the fall?" That question has an answer, and the answer is usually two or three names.

### The test for whether it worked

A week later, can anyone repeat something that was said to them? If the answer is "we got branded water bottles," the day produced an object. If someone can quote a sentence about their own work back to you, it produced recognition. Those cost different amounts and it's not the direction most people assume.

### And skip the all-hands slide

Company-wide statements about how much everyone is valued are the least efficient form this can take, because a message addressed to everyone is addressed to no one. The unit that works is one person writing about one thing another person did.
