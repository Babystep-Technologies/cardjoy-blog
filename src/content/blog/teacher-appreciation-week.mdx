Teacher Appreciation Week runs Monday, May 3 through Friday, May 7 in 2027, with National Teacher Day on Tuesday the 4th.

Here's a thing worth knowing before you plan anything. Ask a teacher who's been in a classroom twenty years what they still have from all those years of end-of-year gifts, and the answer is remarkably consistent: a folder. A box in the closet. A drawer in the desk with notes in it.

Not the mugs. Not the gift cards, which were spent and forgotten within the month. The notes.

### Why the written thing outlasts everything else

Teaching has an unusual feedback structure. You work with a kid for one year, at the end of which they leave, and then you almost never find out what happened. Did the reading click eventually? Did the kid who couldn't sit still turn out fine? Did any of it matter?

The evidence arrives at random and years late, if at all. So a note that says specifically what changed isn't a courtesy — it's data the teacher has no other way to get. That's why the folder exists, and why teachers go back to it on the bad days, which in this profession are numerous.

### What makes a note worth keeping

The keepers are specific and they name a change. Not "you were a great teacher" but "she started reading for fun in March and hasn't stopped." Not "thank you for everything" but "you were the first person who told him he was good at math and he now believes it."

From students, the unpolished ones are the best ones. A parent should resist the urge to edit.

And there's real value in a note from years later — the student who's now twenty-six writing to say the thing they didn't understand at eleven. Teachers describe those as the ones that stop them cold, because that loop almost never closes.

### The rest of the building

Worth remembering during this week: school nurses (Wednesday, May 5), counselors, librarians, and specialists are all part of it, and they're routinely skipped when a class organizes something for the homeroom teacher.
