National Nurses Week runs May 6 through May 12, 2027 — starting on National Nurses Day and ending on Florence Nightingale's birthday, as it does every year regardless of what the calendar is doing.

The scheduling problem is the first thing to understand about recognizing a nursing unit. Twelve-hour shifts, days and nights, rotating patterns, weekends. A single unit is a team whose members may go a month without overlapping. There is no moment when everyone is present.

Which means the standard hospital gesture — a catered lunch in the break room at noon on Tuesday — reaches the day shift on Tuesday. Nights get a note taped to a mostly empty tray. This happens every single year and everyone knows it.

### Anything that requires presence will miss people

That's not an argument against the lunch. It's an argument for making sure the part that carries actual meaning isn't attached to a time and a place, because the people most often missed by the timing are the ones working at three in the morning with the thinnest staffing.

### Families have things to say and no way to say them

The other broken channel runs from patients and families back to the unit. A family spends four days in an ICU waiting room, and one nurse explains what's happening in a way they can actually follow, or notices something at two in the morning, or is simply the person who sat with them for ten minutes on the worst day of their lives.

Then the stay ends, they go home, and there's no mechanism. They don't know the last name, they're not sure it would be appropriate, the hospital survey asks about parking. The most meaningful feedback a nurse could receive routinely evaporates in the parking garage.

### The specifics nurses report keeping

Same pattern as everywhere else on this calendar. Not "thank you for all you do" — that phrase has been printed on so many hospital banners it has gone functionally silent. What gets remembered is the named moment: the night you noticed the change before the monitor did, the way you talked to my father like a person and not a room number, the shift you stayed two hours past the end of.

---

A group card is a reasonable answer to the shift problem specifically: everyone signs on their own hours, nights included, and the unit gets one thing they can keep at the station instead of a lunch half of them missed. It also gives a family a way to send something back after they've gone home — which is usually when they finally have the words.
